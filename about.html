Ecological Forestry

Longleaf pine forests are managed for multiple objectives, including timber production, wildlife habitat, recreation, and aesthetics. Principles of ecological forestry are well-suited for these objectives: they emphasize forest continuity over time, multiple age classes, natural regeneration, and emulation of the natural disturbances that historically structured these forests, such as frequent fire and other small-scale disturbance events. Timber harvests are only one component of implementing ecological forestry.

Selection silviculture, which involves harvests that remove individual trees or small groups rather than clearcutting and replanting even-aged blocks, is a common approach to putting ecological forestry into practice. While its general principles are well established, they aren't defined by a single technique and can be adapted to local conditions, financial constraints, and management goals. One well-documented example is the Stoddard-Neel approach, developed in the Red Hills region of southwest Georgia and north Florida, the same longleaf pine country where the Jones Center at Ichauway sits. The approach traces back to Herbert Stoddard, a wildlife biologist and forester whose early-20th- century bobwhite quail research led him to a broader philosophy of land management, later carried forward and refined by his colleague Leon Neel into a full forestry practice built on single-tree selection and frequent prescribed fire. Read more in the Jones Center's outreach publication on the Stoddard-Neel approach.

Why We Built Pinecraft

Pinecraft exists to close the gap between reading about single-tree selection and begining to pratice it. Marking a stand to meet complex objectives means weighing dozens of judgment calls per acre and having a vision for the future stand. In the field, immediate feedback on tree selection can be slow. You can't fully know how a mark turned out until the harvest is done and the stand has had time to respond.

Practice, Repeated

In VR or on PC, Pinecraft lets you mark a stand, see the results instantly, and try again, as many times as it takes to become familiar with uneven-aged tree selection. Load a real stand you've measured in the field, or generate one with our Stand Generator, and practice ecological forestry in a low-stakes setting before you're marking trees for real.
